A delegation-first operating environment for giving software a job. And being quite particular about what else it may do.

THE ARRANGEMENT

A job should come
with a clear brief.

You already know what you want the tool to do. The question is how much else you have to hand over.

01

Give it a specific job.

A bounded activity defines what software may read, what it may change, and what needs your sign-off first.

02

Keep the scope in view.

The inspector is designed to show what a job can still do, what you approved, and what cancelling would prevent.

03

Get the whole story.

A record separates what was claimed, what was observed, what a service accepted, and what remains unknown.

FAIR QUESTIONS

Still in
the workshop.

The design is written down. A first experiment is under way. Nothing has shipped yet.

Can I try it yet?+

Not yet. We’re testing the design on existing platforms first. This page introduces the project; it isn’t an invitation to hand over an account.

Is this just for AI?+

No. The same rules apply to apps, scripts and AI participants. Diamond Dog is about the arrangement for delegated work, whoever—or whatever—is doing it.

Where will it run?+

The intended environment is an open-source layer on Linux: a headless runtime and an inspector for seeing what a job may still do and what happened. It isn’t a new desktop.

What does cancelling mean?+

The design aims to make clear which future work cancellation would prevent. It cannot promise to reverse something that has already happened in an outside service.
