README HEADER — PROPOSED FOR RENAME, NOT APPLIED NOW
Diamond Dog
a delegation-first operating environment
Delegate work, not your identity.

Diamond Dog is an open-source project exploring how to hand work to software through bounded activities: what a job may read, what it may change, what needs your sign-off, and a record of what happened.

The design is written down and a first experiment is under way. Nothing has shipped. The intended environment runs on Linux as a headless runtime with an inspector. Apps, scripts and AI participants follow the same rules.

Start with the design and the experiment. Contributions that make the arrangement clearer are welcome.

GPLv3 runtime. MIT SDK, interfaces and examples.

VOICE
Honest. Plain-spoken. Wry. Calm. On your side.
Write for a capable non-specialist. Name the concrete action and the limit. Separate a claim, an observation, an outside service's acceptance and an unknown. A record is evidence to inspect; it is not a blanket assurance.

The humour points at overbroad access and unhelpful software conventions. Never mock the user or another project. Personality belongs in the website, README, stickers, docs asides and empty states. Approval screens, uncertain outcomes, payments and personal records stay sober and exact.

Use: access; grant; authority; what it may do; bounded; scoped; participant; an AI participant when relevant; cancel, revoke or compensate only when that is the specific supported action.
State the actual outcome. Preserve distinctions between claimed, observed, accepted and unknown. Do not collapse them into a generic success message.
Use “we're designing,” “we're testing,” and “the experiment” where capability has not been demonstrated. Do not promote the current project into a full system or imply it has shipped.

OUTCOME LANGUAGE — DOCUMENTATION SPECIMENS ONLY
These examples explain voice. They are not replacements for the inspector screens fixed by the evaluation protocol.

Claimed
A participant reports: “[specific result].”
Label the speaker or source. Do not present a participant's report as independent observation.

Observed
Observed: [specific event] at [time], recorded by [source].
Keep the statement within what that observation supports.

Accepted
[Service] accepted [specific request] at [time].
[External result] was not observed.
Acceptance by a service does not establish every later effect.

Unknown
The outcome is unknown. [Specific evidence available]. [Specific evidence missing].
Example: The connection ended before a response was received. The service may have processed the request. Check the service before sending another request.

Action review
Review the exact action
[Specific resource, action, recipient and amount where applicable.]
State what the person is authorising, with no joke and no vague assurance.

Scope explanation
This activity may read [selected resources] and change [selected resource]. [Specified action] needs your sign-off first.
Use only where the implementation actually enforces that arrangement.

Cancellation explanation
Cancelling prevents [specific future work]. [Already accepted external action] may still take effect.
The explanation must follow the actual activity and service state. Do not promise that cancellation reverses an external effect.
